# Case study: four Obsidian vaults, one chat. A reflection

A reflection on one long working session with Claude Code (desktop app, Code tab) that started with the question "how do I stop opening four chats?" and ended with a working meta vault, five cross-vault bridges and a weekly backup. The practice itself is in [08 · Many vaults, one AI chat](08-many-vaults-one-ai-chat.md). This page is about **how we got there**: what worked, what I would do differently, and what I learned about working with an AI agent on a personal knowledge system.

## Starting point

Four Obsidian vaults, about 1,000 notes in total:

| Vault | What's in it | Privacy |
|---|---|---|
| Personal | reflection, life path, sources and research | private |
| Work | an AI program at a company: meetings, processes, methods | NDA |
| Project | an urban-development program: cities, ecosystems, community | internal |
| Speaking | talks, events, decks, story bank, speaker profile | almost public |

The job to be done, in my own words: *"When I find useful material or a new topic, it can be useful to several vaults at once. But I'm not going to open three or four chats, right? Projects in the AI app are basically unconnected Obsidian vaults."* My own first guess was a "Meta_obsidian" vault with meta links, a short context for each vault, scripts and instructions on what goes where, plus a registry of Markdown files with hashes. And a clear constraint: **not everything should be dragged between vaults**.

## How the session went

1. **Research first, two web searches.** "Claude desktop with several Obsidian vaults", then "interdisciplinary connections between different knowledge bases, best practice". The first answered *how* (Obsidian can't link vaults; `obsidian://` URIs work; Claude Code can see several folders; hash registries are a common pattern). The second answered *what is worth linking* (Luhmann's ~2% of cross-section links, boundary objects, the "everything is connected" trap, analogy search by mechanism). The rules in the meta vault come straight from these findings.
2. **Scaffold.** Given the four paths, the agent built the meta vault: `CLAUDE.md` with the rules, one profile per vault (written after reading each vault's own rules file), `config/vaults.json`, the registry script, templates for bridges and digests, a glossary and a decision log.
3. **First digest.** The first scan registered 999 notes as the baseline. The agent read only the notes with a `description` and the recent changes, then proposed exactly five bridges, strongest first, each with "the task it solves in the target vault" in one sentence.
4. **"Save the links too."** I asked to save every source from both searches, *so that another AI, not only this one, can process the meta vault later*. They went into a sources note with a short "what it says" for each and an honest caveat: most sources are blogs and forum posts, and papers were read only as abstracts.
5. **"All five bridges, and AI tools live in the personal vault."** I accepted all five proposals and made a routing decision: general material on AI tools (agents, Claude Code) should live in my personal vault, with bridges from work and speaking. Eight notes moved. At each old location in the work vault, a bridge with **the same file name** was left behind, so the 16 work notes that linked to them still resolve. The work-specific sections ("what applies at work", a draft letter to colleagues) stayed in the work-vault bridge and were cut from the personal copy.
6. **Backup.** "Zip each vault weekly, to Dropbox and Google Drive." Five archives (four vaults + the meta vault), about 35 MB a week, are now registered as a Windows Task Scheduler job for Sunday evening.

## What worked

- **Asking "where does this live?" before "where else could it go?"** The one-home rule removed most of the anxiety. Most material really belongs to one vault.
- **The mechanism test found the best link.** "Citizen engagement" (project vault) and "growing a community of AI practitioners inside a company" (work vault) share no keywords. With the domain words stripped, they are one problem: get people to take part in a change they didn't ask for. A keyword search would never connect them.
- **A hard limit of five.** It forced ranking, and every proposal had to pass the one-sentence test. All five were accepted. I would rather see five good bridges than twenty weak ones.
- **Baseline on the first scan.** Without it the first "what's new" would have been a queue of 999 notes.
- **Reading each vault's own rules before writing.** One vault said "this folder is read-only", another "don't bulk-read transcripts", and in the speaking vault notes with `auto: true` are regenerated by a script. So bridges went into `Ideas/` and into new notes instead of into the auto-generated topic notes, where the next rebuild would erase them.
- **Moving without breaking links.** Leaving a same-name bridge in the old place is a cheap trick: backlinks keep working, and the bridge tells you where the original went.
- **Writing the decision down as a rule.** "AI tools → personal vault" went into `CLAUDE.md` and into the vault profiles. The next session won't ask again.
- **Saving the research with the system.** The rules now come with their reasons and sources. Another agent can re-check them instead of trusting them.

## What went wrong, and what I'd change

- **The agent added security I didn't ask for.** For the NDA vault it switched on archive encryption by default, which required a password in an environment variable, so that vault was simply skipped in the first backup. My reaction was short: *"all archives without a password, come on."* Encryption is now opt-in per vault and off by default. **Lesson:** a sensible default is still a decision. When a "safer" option adds friction or can make the job fail silently, the agent should ask, not assume.
- **Plan the cloud storage before the backup.** Google Drive's desktop client wasn't installed, so the second destination is waiting. The Dropbox account turned out to be on a small free plan, and the local Dropbox folder was already bigger than the quota. Check the free space before you schedule anything.
- **Windows and non-Latin paths need care.** Vault paths with Cyrillic folder names and a user name with a space broke a quick `python -c` one-liner because of backslash escaping. Small scripts in files, `pathlib`, and `stdout.reconfigure(encoding="utf-8")` fixed it. If your folders aren't in English, test the scripts on them first.
- **Large files don't belong in a weekly zip.** A few PPTX decks and one OCR'd PDF were 20–70 MB each. They are skipped and listed in the log, because their originals live elsewhere. Decide this rule up front.
- **The glossary is still a draft.** The agent built it from the profiles. It is useful, but only I can say whether "defending a project to investors" and "selling a topic to an event organizer" really are the same task.

## What I learned about working with an agent on a knowledge system

1. **Start with the job, not the tool.** "Material is useful to several vaults, I don't want several chats" is a better prompt than "set up an Obsidian plugin".
2. **Ask for research before architecture.** Two searches gave the design its rules. Without them it would have been a pile of links.
3. **Keep the human as editor-in-chief.** The agent proposes, I tick boxes. Nothing is written to a vault without a yes. This is the same principle as in [01 · Principles](01-principles.md), applied to knowledge instead of slides.
4. **Make every correction permanent.** A log of decisions plus rules that grow from corrections is how the system learns without fine-tuning.
5. **Privacy is part of the routing.** "Which vault?" and "what is allowed to leave this vault?" are one question. An NDA vault can still give away an anonymized *method*. It never gives away names, numbers or quotes.
6. **Leave a trail for the next AI.** `CLAUDE.md`, `AGENTS.md`, the sources note and the decision log mean the next agent, or a different model entirely, can pick up where this one stopped.

## Numbers from the session

- 4 vaults, 999 notes at baseline, about 1,000 after the session
- 2 web searches → 30 sources saved
- 5 bridges proposed, 5 accepted; 8 notes moved with 16 backlinks preserved
- 5 archives per week (≈35 MB), last 6 kept, files over 20 MB skipped
- 1 correction from me that became a rule, and 1 that changed a default

## Day two: the blind spot

After two days of use I opened the meta vault in Obsidian and saw nothing of what had been done. The bridges were real, but they lived inside the four child vaults, and Obsidian can't link across vaults. The routing log was a table of paths, not links. The fix was a generated **map** inside the meta vault: hubs for vaults and projects, nodes for materials and for every link, including the rejected ones, with NDA titles masked. Back-filled from two days of decisions: 27 materials, 33 links, 13 projects. The full write-up is in [10 · Make the meta vault visible](10-meta-vault-map.md).
